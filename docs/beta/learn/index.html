What this forecast is

Bacteria tests at the mouth of San Pedro Creek are taken about once a week, and the results often take weeks to become public. This experimental forecast is meant to fill that gap.

It estimates how likely the county's weekly ocean test at the creek mouth would be to fail the state standard on a given day, and it sorts days into four levels of risk, from lower than usual to very high.

It does not predict illness, and it never says the water is safe. Even at the lowest level, about one test in three fails.

The forecast is not a measurement, an official advisory or medical advice. Official beach postings come from San Mateo County Environmental Health.

What's being tested, and why

The forecast estimates water quality at the mouth of San Pedro Creek, where the creek meets the ocean at Linda Mar. It is based on the test of the ocean water that San Mateo County takes there about once a week. The county has tested that spot since 2000 and publishes the results, and the waves there are popular with surfers.

The ocean test counts enterococcus, a bacterium that lives in the gut and is a standard marker of fecal contamination. Tests in the creek also count E. coli, another gut bacterium. Most kinds of these bacteria live in the gut without doing harm, but some kinds can make people sick, including the kinds of E. coli behind food recalls. The tests count all kinds together. They are used because these bacteria show that fecal waste is in the water, and fecal waste can carry many other germs that make people sick.

Counts are reported per 100 mL of water. California's standard for a single ocean sample is 104, and a test above it fails. This site calls a result above 312, three times the standard, “far over”.

How the forecast works

The forecast model relies on two things that can be known in advance: how much rain has fallen or is forecast over three days, and how wet or dry the ground is. In the record since 2022, tests failed more often after rain and when the ground was dry.

Input 1
Rain over three days
The rain forecast for the day, plus the rain measured on the two days before. More rain raises the chance of a failed test.
Input 2
Ground wetness
A single number that keeps a running tally of recent rain. Drier ground raises the chance of a failed test.

The ground-wetness number rises after storms and falls through dry spells. It stands in for groundwater, because the valley's monitoring wells have not been read since 2016.

Other inputs were tested, among them air and sea temperature, sunshine, waves, the tide, the time of year and recent test results. None improved the forecast enough to be added.

Dry ground mostly marks the dry season, when the weather is also warm and the creek is low. It is not evidence that dry ground pollutes the water.

How accurate is the forecast?

To check the forecast, each year's tests from 2022 to 2026 were set aside in turn. The forecast was built from the other four years, and then scored on how well it ranked the hidden year's days from lower to higher risk.

0.73
the forecast model
0.55
guessing from the most recent test result
How the score works: take one failed test and one passed test at random. The score is how often the forecast gave the failed one the higher chance. A score of 0.5 is a coin flip and 1 is perfect.

The forecast has real limits. It rests on five years and 243 tests, and how well it does one to three days ahead has not yet been tested.

It does not meet the standard that the published method sets for a yes-or-no advisory, and neither did the most polluted beaches in the original study. This is why the forecast gives levels of risk and not a yes or no.

Every forecast is logged before the test result is known, and later compared with it. The log is public, and it will be used to improve the forecast.

Some tests do come back within the standard: 93 of the 243 since 2022. But no reliable way has been found to forecast which days those will be, and a passing test describes only one sample at one moment.

Why is the water like this?

The forecast says nothing about where the bacteria come from. But the same test records can help with that question.

One long-standing theory, held by people who have worked on the creek for decades, is that leaking private sewer lines in the valley feed the creek. It is not the official position of the Coalition or of any group. The theory has four links. Each is graded here by what the data can and cannot show.

aThe problem does not need rain
Strong
Since 2022, 100 of the 150 failed ocean tests came with under 1 mm of rain in the previous three days. Most days are rain-free, and 56% of the tests taken on them failed, against about 4% in 2000 to 2013. The effect of rain has not changed in 26 years, so this rise is the record's clearest sign that a source independent of rain has grown.
bIt enters the creek in the residential valley
Supported
In the Coalition's tests from July 2024 to September 2026, the typical dry-weather enterococcus level was 212 at Linda Mar Bridge and 218 at Sanchez Adobe, then 363 at Peralta Bridge and 277 at the creek mouth. The jump between Sanchez Adobe and Peralta Bridge rests on under two years of tests.
cIt is human waste
The Coalition's result
The Coalition reports that DNA testing shows human waste. This project holds no DNA data and can neither confirm nor challenge that.
dIt moves with the water table
Suggested, more evidence needed
On dry days since 2022, ocean tests failed more often when groundwater appeared to be above normal for the date than when it was below: 65% against 54%. “Groundwater” here is a stand-in: the dry-weather flow of neighbouring creeks. The evidence so far is limited, and more would be needed to establish this link.

No one measures groundwater in the valley today. Monitoring wells that were read from 2002 to 2016 found it 4 to 6 feet below the surface.

Other explanations
The test records can show that a source independent of rain exists, and roughly where it enters the creek. They cannot show what that source is. Leaking sewer lines are one explanation. Temporary encampments of unhoused people near the creek, lacking sewer service, are another that has been suggested. Encampments are a more recent development, and there is no record here of where they were or when, so that explanation cannot be tested. Evidence of human waste would not separate the two, since both are human sources.

Warmth. Failures rise in the warm months, but those are also the dry months, so the two are hard to tell apart. Warmth may matter in the creek, shows no effect at the ocean station, and is not part of the model.

What people report about their health

To learn how water quality here affects the people who use the beach and the creek, the Linda Mar Water Quality Coalition runs the Linda Mar Health Impacts Survey. It asks swimmers, surfers and beachgoers about symptoms after being in the water.

Help improve it

This is an experimental tool for understanding water quality at Linda Mar. The community deserves clean, safe water, and this is a small contribution toward that.

Version 0.1 is a starting point, and the forecast will be improved. Its data, its code and its limits are public so that others can check it, challenge it and improve it. The work so far points to several leads. None has yet earned a place in the model, but each might with more data:

  • The tide. Tests taken on mornings with a falling tide failed more often: 67% against 58%.
  • Yesterday's rain, measured nearby. When a statistical procedure picked the inputs automatically, rain on the previous day, taken from a rain gauge, was the one it chose most consistently.
  • Faster test results. A fresh result shifts the odds for the next test by about 10 percentage points for two weeks. The county's results become public too late to use.
  • Sunshine. More sun went with fewer failures in some of the checks. The daily log now records forecast sunshine, so a year of entries would allow a proper test.
  • Sea temperature this winter. This winter brings a strong to very strong El Niño on top of a marine heat wave, conditions outside anything in the record. The daily log records sea temperature, which makes this winter a natural test.
Raise a problem or suggest a change
A rain gauge in the valley, measurements of the creek, or faster test results would each make one of these leads testable. If you have a record like that, please get in touch.
